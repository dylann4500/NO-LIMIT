## 1. Summary

- **Measured from raw hands (A):** all 20 opponents. Bay Area: 1,100 tables per team per scored round, us at every
  table. Boston: 360, another Boston team at every table. Pittsburgh: 630, a third team's tables, rounds 2-6 (round 1
  is not in the files).
- **Fifteen of the 20 fold to 4-bets 72% or more in their last round**, and few made them pay: the three fields
  4-bet only 8-16% of the time. The exceptions are Bay Area's three shove re-raisers (Lemonade 1%, AZ 7%, sandylikesfrogs 15%),
  Preesh (46%) and AEPi AK (69%). (A)
- **Fourteen of the 20 react to what a seat has done at the table, and all three regional winners do.** Re-raise a
  seat again once it folds: 2 Curry, Team 123, Du-o, Situational Awareness, 0_o GTO Slop, Pluto, LLinusLLoveBot
  (Magnus RLsen late). Bet into seats that fold: Team 72, Team 123, Du-o, 0_o GTO Slop, BHTG, AEPi AK. Steal from big
  blinds that fold: Pluto (19% against 74%), Team 72, Du-o, BHTG, 0_o GTO Slop, Preesh, Hotwheels. Read who re-raises:
  AZ, Magnus RLsen, AEPi AK, Hotwheels. The rules were in each team's first bots on file, so they are the part of a
  team most likely to come back. (A; C for Sunday)
- **One bot shape appeared at all three regionals:** opens of exactly 2.00bb, re-raises of exactly 3.25x, and the
  re-raise-after-a-fold rule: 2 Curry (Bay Area 1st), 0_o GTO Slop (Boston 5th) and LLinusLLoveBot's first bot
  (Pittsburgh, 2nd after four rounds). Expect it again; section 2 says how to tell them apart. (A; C)
- **The counter-wave is real and late.** At Boston and Pittsburgh the top teams ended the night 4-betting 19-27% and
  folding to 3-bets 54-61%. Cold Decks switched to it in the last round and had the best round-6 score of
  Pittsburgh's top 12; AEPi AK rose from 15.3 to 17.9 as it switched. Bay Area's top stayed at 7% and 68%. (A; `field_three_regionals.md`)
- **Winners keep one family.** 2 Curry, Du-o, Pluto, Lemonade (after one rewrite), BHTG and Hold Em Trophies played
  one family all night; Pluto and 2 Curry were first or second in every round. (A)
- **Against us (A, Bay Area only).** Our points fell at every Bay Area team's table. Beyond what each team's own score
  predicts, the losses that stand out are Team 123's last four rounds (-3.1 ± 0.3 a table), 2 Curry (-1.05 ± 0.25)
  and sandylikesfrogs (-1.11 ± 0.26). Appendix D has the table.
- **Practice rounds:** at Bay Area and Boston most bot changes were first seen in the scored round (8 of 12 and 7 of
  12); at Pittsburgh, where practices were 5,000-10,000 tables, most were in the practice before (6 of 9). AZ, Poker
  is all you need and LLinusLLoveBot played practice bots that matched neither neighbouring scored round. (A)
- **Twist play:** Boston's finalists spent flop-chips in proportion to the pot and kept a third in hand (section 5.2);
  at Pittsburgh every finalist kept pairs and hands good on both boards at the field's rate, and they differed only
  in who bets a river without a hand (section 6.3). (A)
- **Most dangerous on Sunday (C):** the three regional winners, 2 Curry, Du-o and Pluto; then Situational Awareness,
  Cold Decks and Lemonade. Reasons in section 3.

## 2. Recognition table for practice 1

At eight seats (case R) each opposing team sits at about 7 of our 19 opponents' seats, so a 1,000-table practice
file holds about 370 of its tables; with 21 teams at six seats (case F) it is 5 of 20 and about 250.
**Same family** = at least two of its three rates inside the band. **Rewritten** = two or more outside.
Otherwise **unclear**: test again on practice 2. Bands are the team's regional range widened by about 5 points,
because a new variant moves every level. "Tables" is how many of that team's tables give enough chances for a rate
5 points inside the band to be 2 standard errors from its edge (or, for a contrast, 150 chances on each side), at
the regional's 90 hands dealt per seat per table. **In case F** a seat is dealt 105-115 hands, so a team needs about
0.8 times the figure in tables shared with us, but shares 250 of our 1,000 and not 370: in tables of ours every figure
is about 1.1 times larger (`format_finals.md` Appendix C.1). A row marked 250 or more needs most of a 1,000-table
practice in either case, and one above about 310 (case F) or 370 (case R) will not settle in it. Run the cross-tabs A, B, D and H of Appendix C on every team (E as well, for
shove calls): they are the same code for all of them.

## 3. What the finalists have in common, and where our bot differs

**In common** (all 20 opponents, each in its last round; A):

1. **Small opens and no limps.** Sixteen never limp (Lemonade 21%; sandylikesfrogs 6%; Poker is all you need and
   LLinusLLoveBot 4%). Ten most often open to exactly 2.00bb and six to 2.25bb. First-in opens run 23-53% (Lemonade
   8%), median 36%; the small blind opens 68% or more in 17 (median 81%).
2. **Sixteen of 20 re-raise 20% or more** (median 25%; ten at 26% or more). From the big blind the top teams
   re-raise a single open about half the time (the three top groups: 40%, 43%, 54%).
3. **They defend the big blind:** sixteen fold it to an open under 50% (median 37%); two fold it 5% (Team 123,
   0_o GTO Slop).
4. **Fold to a 3-bet runs from 30% to 84%, median 63%.** Seven 4-bet 15% or more, none of them from the Bay Area
   (Du-o, Magnus RLsen, Hotwheels, Cold Decks, Hold Em Trophies, Claude Mythos 5.5, AEPi AK).
5. **Fifteen fold to 4-bets 72% or more** (section 1).
6. **Fourteen react to seats** (section 1). Eight have the re-raise-after-a-fold rule.
7. **Postflop is plain where there is one board:** c-bets 55-95% (median 81%) at Bay Area and Boston, and flop bets
   are raised under 10% of the time by all but five (BHTG 35%, Poker is all you need 20%, sandylikesfrogs 16%,
   LLinusLLoveBot 12%, Team 123 11%). With two boards c-bets fall to 51-68% for six of Pittsburgh's seven.
8. **Short stacks shove, but less than we do:** first-in shoves at 10bb or less 24-63% (median 47%).

**Where the regionals' leaders differ.** Bay Area's leaders opened 39-53% first in (Lemonade aside); Boston's five
23-41% and Pittsburgh's seven 25-38%, and Du-o, Pluto and Poker is all you need opened 5-9% at level 1. Boston's and
Pittsburgh's leaders ended the night 4-betting; Bay Area's did not. `field_three_regionals.md` has the field-level
comparison.

**Most dangerous (C).** The three regional winners, for the same three reasons: each was first or second in almost
every round, each kept one bot family from its first practice to round 6, and each reads seats.

## 4. Bay Area cards

Per-round rates for every team are in Appendix B; seat tests in C; our results in D; practice against scored in E.
"Beyond its score" is our loss at a team's tables after removing what a team of that strength costs an average
opponent (Appendix A). Each card's "use" lines are grade C: the rate is measured, using it is untested.

### 2 Curry (1st: 20.4, 20.2, 21.2, 20.5, 20.4, 20.1)

- **Class:** wide pressure with seat learning, all six rounds. Opens 43-54% (always 2.00bb), 3-bets 32-39% (always
  3.25x, 1% all-in), never limps, flat-calls 1-2%, c-bets 90%.
- **Changed:** small steps every round, never a rewrite: opens 54% to 43%, big blind folds 54% to 32%, turn bets when
  checked to 57% to 77% (round 4), more steals from folding big blinds (rounds 5-6). Practice showed the next scored
  bot in 3 of 4; round 3's changes were not in the practice before it.
- **Within a table:** re-raises a lone opener 18% with no history, 42% after one fold, 7% after one non-fold, 77%
  after three or more with 80%+ folds (n = 48,846; 16,683; 12,802; 17,574). It also reads who re-raises it: it folds
  83% to a seat's re-raise, 65% once that seat has re-raised three times, and then 4-bets 13% (n = 4,932; 4,981).
- **Against us:** 15.9 at its tables against 18.1 elsewhere: -2.21 ± 0.25, of which -1.05 is beyond its score.
- **Use:** (1) Do not fold the first re-raise at a table; 4-bet it. It folded to 80% of 4-bets (n = 10,940; 74% to
  all-in ones in rounds 3-6). (2) Re-raise its 2bb opens: 83% folds (n = 38,630), 88% to all-in re-raises. (3) Raise
  its flop bet: as the first bettor it folded to a raise 63% (n = 3,292); this one depends on the twist.
- **To have removed it:** two thresholds. Our public write-up names its 81% fold, so allow 50% that it folds under 70%
  on Sunday. The seat rule is its edge and will stay.

### Lemonade (2nd: 19.8, 19.3, 20.7, 20.6, 20.1, 19.6)

- **Class:** limp or shove, all six rounds. Open-limps 21-24%, raises first in 8-11% (0% from early and middle seats,
  2% on the button, 46% in the small blind).
- **Changed:** one rewrite before round 3, already in the practice before it: 3-bets 22% to 13% (all-in 20% to 10%),
  flat-calls 9% to 19%, turn bets when checked to 13% to 80%, folds to turn bets 35% to 54%. Then the same bot for
  four rounds.
- **Within a table:** nothing from round 3 on (cross-tabs A, B and D all within 3 points).
- **Against us:** -1.44 ± 0.25; -0.34 beyond its score (not significant).
- **Use:** (1) Its limp is not weak: raised, the limper folds 7% and re-raises 44% (n = 20,581). Raise its limps
  for value only. (2) 72% of its 3-bets are all-in and it folds to a 4-bet 1% (n = 627): never 4-bet as a bluff.
  (3) It folds to 73% of flop bets and raises under 1% (n = 24,988); its own flop bet folds to a raise 79%
  (n = 3,135). Twist-dependent.
- **To have removed it:** these are the design, not leaks. A team that froze a 20-point bot for four rounds will
  probably bring the same plan; expect limps again and nothing to steal from, since it never raises light.

### AZ (3rd: 21.2, 19.4, 17.9, 19.1, 18.0, 17.3)

- **Class:** none of `field.md`'s four. A shove re-raiser with seat reads: 3-bets 14% (11 all-in) in rounds 1-3 and 20%
  (17 all-in) after; folds to a 4-bet 6.5% (n = 872).

## Appendix A: method and limits

- **Data.** The 14 Bay Area files in `data/README.md` (6 scored rounds, about 20,150 tables each; practices 1-4 and
  6-9), one pass each. Every team is seen only at tables where we also sat, about 1,100 a round. Filler seats are
  excluded as actors but count as opponents. Official scores are from `data/results-bay-area/`.
- **Definitions** are `stat_definitions.md`'s, re-implemented in a throwaway script and checked against `field.md`
  Appendix A: the top 20's means match to the point in every round; the whole-field means differ by up to 3 points
  on open and limp rates because this note averages only teams that raise (the default in `stat_definitions.md`),
  where Appendix A averaged all teams seen (the same reason moves flat-calls by 2).
- **Extra rates** used here and not in `stat_definitions.md`: a limper facing a raise (folds, re-raises); the big
  blind re-raising a single open; fold to a 3-bet by its size and by the opener's depth; first-in opens by position
  and by players left; calls of a first-in shove with no callers by players left; and "level 1" versions (hands
  1-20) of the main rates.
- **Seat-history cross-tabs (Appendix C)** bucket a decision by what the other seat had done earlier at the same
  table, counting everything public (not only against this team). They are raw cross-tabs, not regressions: time in
  the table and stack depth are only partly controlled (A restricts to both stacks at 25bb or more and no callers).
  Three checks: the archive's controlled regressions flag the same teams on A, B and D; our own bot is flat on the
  rules it did not have (A, E) and positive on the ones it had (B, H); and each test's row for all other teams gives
  the background. On C, G and E that background is not zero, so only teams well past its 10th-90th percentile range
  count. E (shove calls) is the weakest: late-table play is mixed in.
- **"Beyond its score"** (Appendix D). Our points at a team's tables minus our points at our other tables, per round,
  regressed on the team's official score over all 756 team-rounds: difference = 1.82 - 0.146 x score. A 20-point team
  costs an average opponent of ours 1.1 points. The residual is what is specific to us and that team; its spread
  across all teams is 0.89 a round against sampling noise of 0.65.
- **Bot changes** (Appendix E): for each pair of files, 29 rates compared; "same" = mean squared z under 3.5 and no
  rate moved 4 points at |z| of 4 or more. A practice file holds 35-72 of a team's tables, so only large changes
  show there.
- **Boston** (added in the afternoon): the same pass and the same definitions over the 13 files of section 5.1, with
  the viewpoint team left out of field means. The auction's `auction` and `bid` events are skipped when counting
  streets, so the flop begins at the first board event as at Bay Area. What a seat paid is its flop-chip balance
  before the hand minus after. Bay Area's practice 1 and rounds 1, 3 and 6 were re-run with the same script for
  `field_three_regionals.md`; the top 20's means again match `field.md` to the point.
- **Pittsburgh** (added late afternoon): the same pass over the nine files of section 6.1. Two boards arrive as two
  `board` events per street; a street starts at the first of each pair. The `discard` event is skipped. Showdown
  hands are classed by a small evaluator written for the pass. Counters were added for how opponents answered each
  team (re-raising its opens, folding to its bets), used in section 6.2.
- **Not done:** no controlled regressions, no showdown ranges, no replay of any counter. Nothing here says a counter
  gains points: every "use" line is grade C.
