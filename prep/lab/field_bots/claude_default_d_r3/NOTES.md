# claude_default_d: notes

## Files

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `agent.py` | the bot (`Agent` at module level). Reads the two JSON tables at import. |
| `preflop.json` | 169x169 heads-up preflop equity matrix plus equity vs k random hands. Generated by `gen_tables.py` (eval7 Monte Carlo, about 5 s). |
| `cond.json` | postflop "range conditioning" table, generated by `gen_cond.py` (about 20 s). |
| `gen_tables.py`, `gen_cond.py` | offline generators. The agent never runs them. |
| `dev/` | test tools only, not needed at runtime: an independent simulator (`sim.py`), archetype opponents (`opponents.py`), a push/fold Nash check (`pushcheck.py`), a robustness check for dict/enum SDK objects (`robust.py`), a CPU-time profiler (`cpu.py`) and a hand tracer (`trace.py`). |

## Design

**Time budget first.** The limit is 1 ms per decision, the real machines are 2.5x slower, and the upload test has no time bank. So nothing heavy runs per decision:
- Preflop is pure table lookup. At import, the 169x169 matrix becomes `EQR[hand][x]`: equity vs the top-x% of hands, for x = 1..100%.
- Postflop makes one `eval7.py_hand_vs_range_monte_carlo` call (C code), with 300 to 500 iterations against a pre-shuffled villain range of at most 300 combos. That is about 40 µs. If the decision is already slow, iterations drop to 150.
- ICM (Malmuth-Harville) is computed only for our own seat, and only in all-in or commit spots.
- `gc.freeze()` and a warm-up run at import.
- Measured CPU time per decision (thread time, so OS preemption is excluded): p50 30 µs, p99 136 µs, max 591 µs. Import takes 0.15 s.

**Opponent model.** Memory holds per-seat counters as marshal bytes (about 660 bytes): hands, VPIP, PFR, fold to a preflop raise, 3-bet, postflop bet frequency, fold to a bet, raise when facing a bet, and big preflop jams. Events are ingested incrementally, and replaying a hand after a timeout is harmless. Rates are shrunk toward priors (VPIP .30, PFR .17, fold-to-raise .55, bet .40, fold-to-bet .42).

**Preflop.**
- Each opponent's action becomes a range width, then an equity lookup:
  - Opens use PFR scaled by position (the number of players behind them).
  - 3-bets and 4-bets use the observed re-raise rate, with floors of 5%, 3.5% and 2.5%.
  - Shoves use a Nash-like width, `4.6 / stackBB^0.9 / behind^0.7`, floored by the seat's observed jam rate.
- At 13 BB effective or less, the bot plays push/fold by ICM expected value. Each player behind gets a calling probability of about `2.0 / callBB^0.8`, adjusted for their looseness. Checks: heads-up at 10 BB it shoves 69% of hands (Nash about 58%) and calls 38% (Nash about 37%). Heads-up at 5 BB it shoves 66% and calls 79%.
- Deeper than 13 BB:
  - Opening ranges depend on position (13% under the gun up to 45% in the small blind). Steals widen when the players behind fold often.
  - Limpers get isolated.
  - Facing a raise, the bot 3-bets when its equity vs the raiser's range is at least 0.57 to 0.62. It flat-calls with a pot-odds check that includes equity realization (0.78 out of position, 0.92 in position) and implied odds for pairs and suited connectors. Otherwise it folds.
  - Any re-raise that would put in a third of the stack or more becomes a shove.
- All-in calls (or calls that commit the stack) use the ICM required equity `(U_fold - U_lose) / (U_win - U_lose)`. AA and KK never fold preflop unless the ICM requirement is above 0.85.

**Postflop.**
- E is the Monte Carlo equity vs the main villain's preflop range, raised to a multiway exponent when there are more opponents.
- E is then conditioned on the villain's line using `cond.json`. That table gives equity vs the top-b% of a range ranked by board strength, which is how equity-threshold bots choose to bet. b comes from the villain's observed bet or raise frequency, adjusted for bet size and earlier-street bets and calls. The result is blended 55/45 with the raw E (w = 0.55, tuned).
- Draw floors apply: flush draw, open-ended straight draw, gutshot, and combo draws.
- Unbet pots: the bot compares EV(check) = E·pot·realization with EV(bet s) for each size s:
  `P(all fold)·pot + P(call)·(E_vs_calling_range·(pot+2B) − B) − (risk of being raised)`.
  The fold probability comes from each seat's observed fold-to-bet rate, scaled by bet size. This one rule covers value bets, thin value, c-bets and bluffs, and it adapts to folders (for example opponents that time out) and to calling stations.
- Facing a bet: raise for value when the conditioned equity is at least 0.70 (0.75 on the river). Call when `Ec·realization + implied ≥ pot odds`. Use ICM when calling would commit the stack.

**Safety.**
- Every decision is wrapped. On any exception the bot returns a legal check, or a fold, and still saves its memory.
- Raise totals are ints clamped to `[min_amount, max_amount]`. Any target at or above 80% of the maximum becomes a shove.
- Action kinds, statuses, seats and events are normalized, so SDK objects work whether they are attributes or dicts, enums or strings (tested in `dev/robust.py`).

## How it was tested

1. `play.py` (stock harness) during development, to check legality, the upload test and latency.
2. `dev/sim.py`: an independent 8-seat SNG simulator written from the README (side pots, blind schedule, hand cap with ICM split, events and history). It runs against archetype opponents:
   - `tag`, `lag`, `nit`, `station`, `maniac`, `shover`
   - `flaky` (a TAG that loses half its decisions to timeouts)
   - `cof` / `minr`, imitations of `check_or_fold` / `min_raiser`
   
   For A/B tests the candidate and the previous snapshot sat at the same tables (`--me2`), over 2,100 tables per field.
3. Bugs found and fixed through this loop:
   - The ICM bust payout was 0 instead of the payout for the place we finish in. That made the bot far too tight 3-handed and heads-up. Heads-up push/call was about 1% before the fix.
   - Shove ranges ignored the shover's position, so the bot called far too tight against small-blind and button shoves.
   - The bot 4-bet to 38% of its stack and then folded to a 5-bet shove. Re-raises that commit a third of the stack now shove.
   - A home-made rank model made the bot overfold to c-bets. It was replaced with the calibrated `cond.json` table.

A/B results (mean points per table, fair share 12.5; "me" is the new version):

| change | archetype field | value-heavy field | 3 vs 3 self-play field |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICM bust fix + positional ranges | +1.0 | n/a | +1.0 |
| more value-betting (lower check realization) | −0.8 | n/a | +0.05 (rejected) |
| wider opening ranges | 0.0 | n/a | 0.0 (rejected) |
| conditioning blend w 0.85 → 0.70 | +0.5 | n/a | +0.4 |
| w 0.85 → 0.55 | +1.3 | +4.2 | +1.4 (kept) |
| w 0.55 → 0.40 | −1.5 | +0.4 | −0.2 (rejected) |
| push/fold threshold 13 BB → 16 BB | −1.6 | 0.0 | +0.3 (rejected) |

The wall-clock tail in that run (p99.9 14 ms, 2,030 decisions over 1 ms, no timeouts) came from the shared machine, not from the bot. The machine's load average was 50 to 110 on 15 cores, and a do-nothing probe bot showed 8 ms spikes under the same load. Thread CPU time for our decisions: p99 136 µs, max 591 µs.

In the dev simulator, one seat of the final bot in a 7-archetype field scores about 24 to 25 points per table, and about 27 in a nit/TAG-heavy field (fair share 12.5).

## Known limitations and ideas
- When a villain bets several streets, their per-street bet frequencies are multiplied as if independent, which over-narrows their range. The w = 0.55 blend partly compensates. A better fix is to learn barrel frequencies directly.
- There are no deliberate 3-bet bluffs and no check-raise bluffs.
- Showdown information (`show` events) is not used yet. It could tell value-heavy bettors apart from bluffers.
- The archetype opponents are guesses at the real field. The parameters were chosen only when they held up across three different fields.

# Round 2 version (`claude_default_d_r2`)

The round-1 result was 14.93 points per table (5th of 22; fair share 12.5). Places 1..8: 153 / 87 / 84 / 88 / 89 / 82 / 96 / 121. Win rate was 19.1% and top-3 rate was 40.5%. 0 failed decisions, and 0.22% of decisions went over the limit.

## Analysis (scripts in `dev/`, run on `../claude_default_d/round1/hand_history.jsonl.gz`)
- `analyze.py` gives chip flow by level, by number of players and by line, plus the list of busts.
  - Level 0 (10/20, deep stacks): −552 BB. Every later level was positive.
  - River showdowns lost: −3,081 BB. River showdowns won: +2,503 BB.
  - Busts: 94 total, 20 of them 8-handed. Many were coolers (77 vs AA, TT vs AQ, AA vs Q6s), and several were multi-street bluffs with BB-defend junk into a preflop raiser that called down.
- `lines.py` gives flop-line results by made-hand class.
  - Flop leads and c-bets are break-even or profitable: air leads +0.18 BB/hand over 381 hands, value bets +4.7 to +6.8 BB.
  - Not a leak.
- `river.py` and `calls.py` cover turn/river decisions.
  - Whole-hand results after calling with a pair look awful: turn −9.2 BB/hand over 85 hands, river −10.2 over 29.
  - Measured at the moment of the call, though, the calls are +EV. River calls with a pair won 38% of hands when the pot odds needed 23%. Two pair won 40% vs 23%. Turn pair calls won 33% vs 24%. The whole-hand losses are just the cost of losing showdowns.
  - Calls against a raise of our flop bet won 56% vs 30% needed.
  - Deep preflop flat calls are break-even: −0.05 BB/hand over 778 hands.

## Hypotheses tested and rejected on the real data
1. "We call too much on the turn and river": rejected, the calls beat their pot odds.
2. "Raises of our bets are pure value": rejected, the calls won 56% on the flop.
3. "Deep preflop flat calls bleed": rejected, they are break-even.

I first implemented all three, then reverted them after the direct pot-odds check above.

## Change kept
- When facing a bet on the river, the conditioning blend drops from w = 0.55 to w = 0.42. That trusts raw equity a little more, so the bot calls rivers somewhat lighter.
- Reason: our river bluff-catches beat their pot odds by about 15 points (about 2.4σ combined over 54 calls), so marginal river folds with pair or two pair were likely losing value against this field.
- This is the only diff from the round-1 version.

## Local results for r2
- `play.py`, 300 tables, seed 7: upload test passed (10 decisions, 0 failed, no illegal actions).
  - self 20.49 (se 0.59), check_or_fold 8.52, min_raiser 0.50.
  - That run logged 12 timeouts. They came from machine load (load average above 50, max wall 135 ms, while our CPU p99 is about 140 µs). The code's cost is identical to round 1, which had 0 failures in the real round.
- Dev simulator, r2 vs the round-1 version at the same tables (1,400 tables): −2.0 (se ≈ 0.8) on the archetype field and −0.5 (se ≈ 0.4) on the self-play field.
  - The simulator's archetypes value-bet rivers harder than the real field does, so I give the real-field data more weight.
  - The disagreement is real, though. If the team prefers the simulator's evidence, submitting the round-1 version unchanged is the conservative choice.

# Round 3 version (`claude_default_d_r3`)

The round-2 result for r2 was 14.26 points per table (7th of 22; round 1 was 14.93, so no measurable change). Places 1..8: 156 / 81 / 59 / 86 / 76 / 90 / 110 / 142. Win rate was 19.5% and top-3 rate was 37%. 0 failed decisions.

## What cost points
- Across both rounds the problem is variance, not win rate. We win as often as the leaders (about 19 to 20%), but finish 8th about 1.7 to 2 times as often (142 vs 71 for the leader in round 2) and 2nd/3rd less often.
- Round-2 busts: 101 total, 52 of them with 7 or 8 players left. The deep-stack early busts are almost all the same line: we bet the flop or turn with one pair, an overpair, or a board-paired "two pair", the villain raises, and we call (often stacking off on the next street). The opponent showed a set, two pair or a better kicker.
- Calls against a raise of our own bet (`dev/replay.py` and the inline analysis):
  - Round 2, flop, one pair, big raise: won 20% vs 33% needed, −51 BB per hand.
  - Round 2, flop, one pair, small raise: −10.6 BB per hand, because we kept calling on later streets.
  - Board-paired two pair was negative in both rounds.

## Change (the only diff from r2)
- **Facing a raise of a bet** (postflop): villain's range is now modeled as the top 12% (flop) or 8% (turn/river) of their range by strength. The calibrated conditioning is trusted at w = 0.92 instead of 0.55.
- **A raise on an earlier street** now carries forward: it multiplies later-street range strength by 0.25 and also triggers the w = 0.92 conditioning. Before, a raise counted only like an ordinary bet.
- Plain bets and the round-2 river bluff-catching change are untouched.

## Validation
- **Replay on real data:** `dev/replay.py` rebuilds every round-1 and round-2 postflop spot where we faced a raise after our own bet and asks r2 and r3 for their decision.
  - r3 folds in 66 hands where r2 continued.
  - Folding would have cost only the chips already invested, so the estimated saving is +640 BB over 1,600 tables, counted once per hand.
  - Those spots are concentrated in deep early pots, which are the 8th-place finishes.
- **Dev simulator, r3 vs r2 at the same tables (1,400 tables):** +2.2 on the archetype field (25.11 vs 22.93; se 0.58 each) and +1.0 on the self-play field (14.69 vs 13.66; se 0.29 each).
- **`play.py`, 300 tables, seed 7:** upload test passed (10 decisions, 0 failed, no illegal actions). self 21.26, check_or_fold 7.07, min_raiser 0.42.
  - That run logged 38 timeouts because the shared machine's load average was about 108 (wall p99 1.8 ms).
  - The bot's own CPU time is unchanged: p50 30 µs, p99 148 µs, p99.9 211 µs.
  - In the real rounds the bot had 0 failed decisions.
